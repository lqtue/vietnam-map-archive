# Thinking Spatially About the Past
### A one-page guide to spatial history — and what it lets you ask

> **The big idea:** History usually asks *when*. Spatial history also asks *where* — and treats "where" as evidence. Space is not a neutral backdrop; it is produced by people, it changes over time, and it shapes what people can do.

---

## 1. The core claims of the field

- **Space is socially produced.** Official, planned space (maps, city plans) and lived, experienced space rarely match. *(Henri Lefebvre)*
- **Space is "always under construction"** — made of relationships, never finished. *(Doreen Massey)*
- **The "spatial turn"** reasserted space alongside time across the humanities. *(Edward Soja)* The term *spatial history* was coined by **Paul Carter** (1987).
- **Maps are arguments, not mirrors.** Every map includes some things, erases others, and serves whoever made it — the state, the colonizer, the tax collector. *(J.B. Harley)*

## 2. How spatial historians actually work

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Historical GIS (HGIS)** | Georeference an old map onto real coordinates → trace features into data → link records (census, property, events) → overlay & analyze change over time |
| **Deep mapping** | Layer qualitative, sensory, contested material (memories, photos, oral histories) onto place |
| **Geographical text analysis** | Extract place-names from large bodies of text and map them |

## 3. What questions can spatial thinking answer?

| Question type | Classic example |
|---|---|
| **Patterns / clustering** | Cholera deaths clustered around one pump (Snow, 1854) |
| **Hidden causes** | The Dust Bowl tracked drought + soil, not just "greedy farmers" (Cunfer) |
| **Who was erased** | Reconstructing voters that commercial maps left off (DeBats) |
| **Change over time** | How a neighborhood's boundaries shifted, and in whose interest |
| **Constraint / possibility** | What a general could actually *see* (Knowles, Gettysburg viewshed) |
| **Lived vs. official space** | "Chinatown" looked segregated on paper but was porous in practice (Lutz) |

**The unifying lesson:** *spatial thinking surfaces claims the text-only archive hides.*

## 4. The field is alive — current projects

**Stanford Spatial History Project** · **ORBIS** (the Roman world measured in travel time) · **CHGIS** (China Historical GIS) · **SODUCO** (mapping Paris from city directories) · **Locating London's Past** · **Allmaps** (open map georeferencing).

## 5. Our project: the Vietnam Map Archive (VMA)

VMA is a working spatial history of colonial Saigon. It **instantiates the theory**:

- Holds the **colonial planners' maps** *and* **lived community memory** as separate, comparable layers (Lefebvre).
- Re-reads **French colonial cadastral maps** as instruments of property and power (Harley).
- Builds a **knowledge graph** of owners, families, and events — relational space, not just coordinates.
- Models places with **start/end dates** — the city as always under construction (Massey).

**What VMA can do:** georeference historical Saigon maps · vectorize building footprints · build a knowledge graph · crowdsource with volunteers · layer across time.

**What VMA asks:** *How did successive planning regimes encode spatial inequality into Saigon's built form — and does that inheritance still shape the city today? Who was erased from the colonial cadastre? Did the planned colonial city match the lived one?*

---

### Three questions to try on any historical map
1. What does this map want you to believe — and whom does it leave off?
2. What would you turn into data — and what would that choice hide?
3. What could this map answer that a text never could?
