# 1898 Saigon Plan: preparing its OCR for review, and why the road-number rule does not apply to it

This record covers the 1898 *Saigon Plan* (`20ec4f9a-16bd-4895-a593-40c6ed9c9555`, 16267 × 14859 px at 0.338 m/px, 1:4000, BnF
Gallica), read from `ocr_labels` on 2026-10-06. It asks whether to review the sheet's one existing OCR run or to re-run it with a
prompt that keeps road numbers (`Rue X No. 32`) attached to street names, as the 1882 review proposed. Everything here is read-only:
no database write, no `ocr/apply`, no OCR call and no Gemini call. The counts come from `work/ocr/scripts/review_patterns.py`
(unchanged) and a new `work/ocr/scripts/prep_unreviewed.py` that does for an unreviewed sheet what the first does for a reviewed
one; the price comes from `ingest_plan.py`, which I read first and which makes only Supabase and public IIIF GETs. **The finding
that matters: 1898 prints no road numbers at all, so the 1882 road-number rule has nothing to act on here. Its street names are
fragmented a different way: the generic `Rue` is printed apart from the name.** Recommendation at the end: review the existing run.

## The run

One run, `2026-09-13T1036-20ec4f9a`, 472 rows, all `pending` (0 validated, 0 rejected, 0 manual, 0 text groups), model
`gemini-3.8-flash`, prompt `seq-v1`, 82 distinct tiles. Locally it is two offset grid passes (`-a`, `-b`, 2400 px tile, 300 px
overlap, 2400 px render, `main_map` crop 439,401,15388,14012) merged under one `run_id`: 41 calls, 214,456 input and 812,357 total
tokens, **$2.36** by `pricing.py` (output taken as total − input, thinking included). Categories: street 186, institution 117,
place 71, hydrology 32, building 28, other 18, legend 12, title 8.

**Letter spacing is not usable here.** 1882's median was 1.23, but those were human-drawn boxes. 1898's model boxes are not tight
text rectangles: `Quai Francis Garnier` is a 115 × 177 px box over a line printed some 600 px long, and the 66 solvable rows spread
over a factor of twenty. The grouping counts below therefore use one letter height (50 px, the median solvable short side) and
`1.2 × characters × h0` lengths, and take only the box centres and rotations from the model. Treat them as indicative.

**The zero for road numbers is not an artefact of the model.** On 1882 the model dropped the number in 19 of 22 cases, so a zero in
its output alone would prove little. I looked at the scan itself: six crops from the IIIF source at native or near-native scale
(centre, the Cholon road and rail junction, the Pellerin/Larclauze/Mac-Mahon crossings, the Citadelle and Port de Saïgon edge, the
legend and title block). Roughly twenty-five street names are readable in them (`Rue Catinat`, `Boulevard Charner`, `Rue d'Ormay`,
`R. Doudart de Lagrée`, `Rue Vannier`, `Francis Garnier`, `Rue Mac-Mahon`, `Pellerin`, `Larclauze`, `Richaud`, `Laubat`,
`Quai de Cholon`, `Primauguet`, `Dupré` and others) and **none carries a number**. The sharpest test is the same street on both
sheets: the 1882 journal cites `Rue Vannier` + `N° 5`, `Quai Charner No. 18` and `Rue Dayot No. 5`, and in these 1898 crops
`Vannier`, `Charner` and `Dayot` are printed with no number. Crops, as `IIIF region x,y,w,h` in source pixels (they will not
survive as files): centre 8800,7800,1800,1400 (Catinat, Charner, Vannier); Cholon junction 7600,9000,2000,1500 (Dayot, Mac-Mahon);
Pellerin/Larclauze 5200,5200,2000,1500; Citadelle and port edge 10000,6000,2100,1500; legend 13300,7800,2400,1900; title block
3500,11000,2100,1500. Four of the six are street crops, all in the central and southern built-up area; the outer districts, the
Cholon side and the north were not looked at. So the statement is: no road number in a sample of the dense core, none in 472
model rows, and the same streets that carry numbers on 1882 carry none here; not a proof that none exists anywhere on the plate.
(`Rue Bourdais`, 1882 `No. 32`, at about 8316,9370, was not in a crop and is the obvious fourth check.)

## What is actually wrong with the 1898 street rows

The scan shows how 1898 prints a street: the generic and the name are separate words laid along the road, often a street width
apart (`Rue` at one corner, `Catinat` further along; `Rue` … `Mac` … `Mahon`). The model returned:

| street rows (186) | n |
|---|---|
| generic + name (`Rue Catinat`) | 90 |
| bare generic (`Rue` 48, `Boulevard` 5, `Quai` 2, `Route` 2) | **57** |
| name with no generic (`Pagès`, `Lafont`, `Mayer`) | 30 |
| connective or trailing connective (`de`, `la`, `Route d'`, `Rue de l'`) | 9 |

116 name rows (of 218 street and hydrology rows) look incomplete by the 1882 test (bare generic, trailing connective, no generic).
**41 of the 57 bare generics sit at exactly 0.9 and 12 at 0.95; only 4 are below 0.9**, so a confidence queue skips almost all of
them. That is the 1882 lesson again (confidence is a weak signal) with a different cause.

Fragment candidates by the 1882 rule (Δrot ≤ 45°, perpendicular offset ≤ 1.5 h, gap ≤ 30 h, candidate with smallest offset wins):

| | n |
|---|---|
| incomplete pieces with at least one candidate | 50 of 116 (mean 1.5 candidates each) |
| suggested pairs (top-1, unordered) | **36**; 14 are mutual |
| of which generic + name-only (the join the reviewer wants) | 5 |
| of which generic + a row that already starts with a generic | 18 |
| other (connective, hydrology, mixed) | 13 |
| bare generic whose centre lies inside a longer row that contains the same word | 18 |

Reading the last two lines together: a bare `Rue` next to or inside `Rue Catinat` is the model's own second copy of the one printed word, which
the reviewer rejects, not a piece to join. Only about five suggestions are the real fragment join. The rule scored precision 0.60,
recall 0.50 on 12 in-sample 1882 pairs, and here it also rests on box lengths I had to assume, so do not read 36 as a forecast of
review work; read it as "suggestions exist for about half the incomplete pieces".

## Legend

**Yes, a legend is printed, unnumbered.** The triage layout (`maps.triage.regions`, model 0.95, human-reviewed 2026-09-10) holds a
`legend` region at x 13501, y 7994, 2033 × 1604 ("Color key legend titled 'Légende'"), and the crop confirms it: the title
*Légende*, five italic lines `Propriétés domaniales affectées aux services milit.res et marine`, `… au service local`, `… non
affectées`, `Propriétés communales`, `Propriétés particulières`, each with a colour swatch, and a note giving the city limits
(`l'arroyo de l'Avalanche, la Rivière de Saigon, le rach Bân Dôn … le rach Bung Binh`). No line is numbered, so there is no
`"12. Name"` list and no index. In the OCR rows: 0 `Propriétés` rows, 0 rows with a centre inside the legend region, 0 numbered
lines; the 12 `legend`-category rows are margins and title-block text (`Echelle de 1/4000`, `800 Mètres.`, `N O R D`, `S U D`,
`OUEST`, `1898.`, the credits and two port-limit notes). The legend text was therefore not read by this run. The layout also holds
`title`, `scale_bar` (at 1708,12659) and `stamp` regions. Reading the five legend lines would take one tile call; nothing in the
review depends on it.

## The prompt change, drafted and not applied

The prompt is `PROMPT_SEQ_V1` in `work/ocr/scripts/prompt.py` (row-sequence calls append `sequence_frame_rules(n)`). Two things
are already in it: a **WHOLE LABELS** block that asks for `"Rue" … "de" … "Genouilly"` as one extraction with one box, and a
**NUMBERED STREETS** block that treats `"N°29"` as *a street label of its own*, not as a tail of the street name. That second block
may contribute to 1882 road numbers arriving as separate boxes (the 1882 truth is half `v1b`, which predates `seq-v1`, so this
is not isolated). Change A is the edit the 1882 review asked for. Following the `seq-v1-idx`
precedent it should be a new key, `seq-v2-road`, so `seq-v1`, the measured gate, does not move and its result cache stays warm.

```diff
--- PROMPT_SEQ_V1
+++ PROMPT_SEQ_V2_ROAD   (A: road numbers; only for a sheet that prints them)
-**NUMBERED STREETS:**
-"N°29", "N° 7", "No. 12" etc. are valid street labels (category="street"), NOT parcel numbers. \
-Extract them when placed along a road centreline. Do NOT extract bare integers inside plot areas.
+**ROAD NUMBERS:**
+A French street name is often followed by its road number: "Rue Bourdais No. 32", "Rue Thabert N° 4". \
+The number belongs to the street label. Return the name and the number as ONE extraction with ONE \
+bbox enclosing both, text exactly as printed ("Rue Bourdais No. 32"). It is the road's administrative \
+number, not a house or parcel number. If the number is printed so far from the name that one box \
+would be mostly empty, return it as its own extraction (category="street", text "No. 32") and leave \
+the name as a separate extraction. Do NOT extract bare integers inside plot areas.
```

For 1898, change A would change nothing: there are no road numbers to keep. The 1898 failure is the other block, and a candidate
for it (B, untested, for the same new key) is:

```diff
 parcel; all of those words are one label with one bbox enclosing every word. Only when a \
 label genuinely runs off the outermost edge of what you are shown do you return the visible \
 part, with notes "continues outside [edge] edge".
+A bare "Rue", "Boulevard", "Quai" or "Route" is never a complete label: the name is printed \
+further along the same road, often a street-width away. Return generic and name as one \
+extraction. Never return the same printed word twice.
```

B asks the model to do what the WHOLE LABELS block already asks, and the first run ignored that for 57 rows (the WHOLE LABELS block entered `prompt.py` on 2026-09-08, before the 2026-09-13 run), so do not
expect it to work unmeasured. Measure it on 1882, where there are 212 validated rows, with `eval.py ocr --pred-run-dir`.

## Recommendation: review the existing run, do not re-run

1. **No street lost a road number**, because none is printed (0 of 186, and none in six scan crops). The re-run's only stated
   purpose has no target on this sheet. This also qualifies rule 1 of the 1882 transfer rules: "applies to French-era sheets"
   should read "applies to a sheet that prints road numbers, checked per sheet". 1882 prints them; 1898 does not. 1895, 1923 and
   1942 are unchecked. Rule 2 (fold `N°` to `No.`) has nothing to fold here.
2. **The only re-run worth making is prompt B, and it is unmeasured.** B asks for what the prompt already asks and the first
   run did not do it. Score it on 1882 (212 validated rows) before spending on 1898. If it were run on 1898 the 472 pending rows
   could be dropped first, but nothing would then be left to compare it with: the existing run is the baseline for B, and a
   second run kept alongside it would bring back 1882's cross-run duplicates (133 of 313 rejections).
3. **The price does not decide it.** One pass is $0.07 to about $1 and the first run was $2.36; the cost of a wrong prompt is
   review time, not dollars. Revisit after the first review gives 1898 its own truth to score B against.

**Review order** for the 472 rows:

1. **Confidence below 0.9: 70 rows** (street 20, place 19, institution 12, hydrology 10, other 7). Cheap and mostly worth a look,
   but it misses almost every bare `Rue` (only 4 of 57 are below 0.9).
2. **Fragments, regardless of confidence:** the 116 incomplete name pieces, led by the 36 suggested pairs. For the 18 bare generics
   inside a longer row that already contains the word, reject the bare piece; for the 5 generic + name-only pairs, group them; the
   rest by hand. 25 of the 116 are also in queue 1, so the union of queues 1 and 2 plus the 18 `other` rows is **172 of 472 rows
   (36%)**.
3. **Detached road numbers to merge: none.** Skip this step on 1898.
4. Last, the 12 `legend`-category and 8 `title` rows, which are margins and the title block: validate or reject in bulk.

## What this does not show

Six crops are a sample, not the sheet. The grouping counts rest on assumed lengths (above) and on a rule fitted to twelve 1882
pairs. The price columns disagree and none is measured for 1898 at the new grids. `ingest_plan.py` counts tiles and calls, not
tokens; the token column is extrapolated from one run at a different render size. No verdict exists on any 1898 row, so every
statement about what the reviewer will reject is a prediction.
