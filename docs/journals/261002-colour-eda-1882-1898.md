# Colour and feature EDA on 1882 and 1898 — what colour can and cannot separate

**2026-10-02/03.** Exploratory only: four read-only analyses (scripts and figures in the session
scratchpad, not in the repo) over the `sheet_features.py` layers. Every `seen:false` window,
the furniture and everything outside the neatline were masked. Most class labels are the river
and road passes' own proposals, which are noisy, so the cell-level numbers are optimistic. The
rows marked **B** rest on by-eye boxes and do not.

## Findings

| Question | 1882 | 1898 |
|---|---|---|
| Paper variation | uniform: sd 0.007 OD, no gradient, so a fixed ink cut is safe (green wash at ~0.05 OD against `MIN_CONC` 0.03 is marginal) | not measured |
| River ripple vs military-blue hatch: same ink? | yes, OD direction 0.8–0.9° apart (hue AUC 0.51–0.62) **B** | yes, 1.8° apart (a control hatch colour sits 24° away, so hue is resolvable) |
| Does ink weight separate them? | yes: ripples ~2x darker (peak OD 0.21 vs 0.10), pixel AUC 0.94 | **no**: pixel AUC 0.55, ridge \|OD\| 0.47 vs 0.44 |
| What separates them | line weight, spacing, coherence | spacing (cell AUC 0.99) and line density |
| River vs military blue, 32 px cells | hue only 0.74–0.90, + tone 0.995, + texture 0.998; boundary cells 0.75 → 0.954 → 0.983 | — |
| Military blue vs salmon, river vs free space | 1.0, 0.998 on hue alone | — |
| Line direction (`theta`) | added nothing | — |
| Building hatch vs land hatch | darker (\|OD\| ~0.69 vs 0.33), **not** bluer; 9/10 predicted-building boxes darker, 0/10 bluer. An earlier hint that buildings are bluer (12 boxes, one window) is refuted | 1 building, no conclusion |

Colour is a rule-out feature (salmon, black ink), not a classifier. Weight is a 1882-only lever;
`river_pass.py` already uses spacing, so it needed no change.

## OSM centrelines against the 1882 road pass v3

Agreement with a noisy mask, not accuracy. Non-service OSM centreline pixels inside `road.png`:
**16.6%** (6.9% by chance); within 6 / 12 / 34 px: 24 / 30 / 46%; median distance to the nearest
road pixel 44 px (15 m). The cause is georeference drift, not the road pass: local best shifts
run up to 76 px (26 m), and the y-offset grows ~13 px per 1000 px along x (~0.7°, R² 0.82).
Agreement reaches 82% in the centre and 0% over water, in the outskirts and on post-1882 grids;
shifting each body cell lifts it from 24% to 45%. No 1898 number: there is no 1898 road mask.

## Local OSM warp onto 1882

Fitted to raw sheet evidence, not to `road.png` (`work/analysis/osm_warp/`). The georeference
error is smooth: ~0.88° rotation and +0.13% scale, so it is best fixed in the georeference itself.
The warp is reliable only in the west and centre-west (~51% of streets `trusted` in
`streets_warped_1882.geojson`), so it is not yet an independent road label source.

## Dark edge lines: plot outline, kerb, road edge

Classes were assigned by rules (partner line, wash on each side), so only 4–11% of lines got
one; most street-edge lines stay unsure. Partner-line AUCs (0.95–1.0) and class hue differences
are circular, so ignore them.

- The three look alike: median width 0.73 m (1882), ~0.8 m (1898); peak OD ~0.4; any single
  feature separates a pair at AUC ≤ 0.73.
- Hue follows the adjacent wash (−0.03 next to blue, −0.11 next to salmon), not the line.
- The one non-circular cue is the shadow side: lines whose open side faces image-down are thick
  (0.87 m / 1.04 m), up-facing thin (0.72 / 0.79), kerbs thin either way. Outline vs kerb by
  width: AUC 0.79 / 0.86 on thick-side lines, 0.57 / 0.58 on thin-side.
- Multi-feature, spatial CV (1882 / 1898): outline vs kerb 0.81 / 0.92, outline vs road edge
  0.79 / 0.81, kerb vs road edge 0.65 / 0.87.
- 1898 has ~9x more street-parallel hatch (33.9k vs 3.7k chunks), a hazard for road-edge rules.

For the road pass: use shadow-side thickness plus which side the fill is on, never colour.
