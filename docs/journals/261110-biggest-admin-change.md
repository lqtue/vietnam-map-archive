# Is 2025 the biggest administrative change in Vietnam's history? — 2026-11-10

Bet 1c (the 2025 break is the lead case). Idea gate: **smallest test** — stock of units by tier at
dated points, share removed per event, ranked. **Kill** — any earlier event that matches or beats
2025 on a tier drops "biggest" for that tier. Work is a first pass; nothing here is cleared for
application text until each cell has a page image (claims ledger).

## Question

Can we say 2025 was the largest change in the number of administrative units in Vietnam's
history, and on which tier?

## Answer so far

**The kill may have fired at the commune tier: 1946–50 is a real rival, and the data cannot yet rank
it against 2025.** Province and district tiers favour 2025. 1976–79 is ruled out at the commune
tier (below). Do not write "biggest" for communes until the pre-1946 stock is counted.

**1946–50 (the rival).** Ân vol. I (p. 464, "Hợp xã nhỏ thành xã lớn") says that under French rule
the three regions had "over 23,000 xã (more than twice today's)", and that after the January 1946
elections most provinces merged small communes into large ones. *Lịch sử Việt Nam* vol. 10 (p. 435)
gives 5,362 xã nationwide for the Democratic Republic in 1949, and says commune area and population
"doubled" by merging, then many large communes were split back to their old size. Read at face value,
23,000 → 5,362 is −77%, which beats 2025's −67%. Read through "doubled", it is about −50%, which
does not. The two numbers do not match, so the 1949 figure probably covers only what the Republic
administered, and the 23,000 probably counts làng and thôn. Neither is a clean stock. The answer
sits between −50% and −77%, and 2025's −67% is inside that range.

**The 1943 baseline (found, second source).** *Annuaire statistique de l'Indochine*, 11th vol.
1943–46 (cited by a vietnamhoc.net study of Annam villages) counts làng: Tonkin 10,687; Annam 11,005
(coastal 7,601 plus the highland provinces); Cochinchina 1,339. Total **23,031**, which matches Ân's
"over 23,000", so the two are one count. A lower, older estimate (National Archives site, early
twentieth century) counts xã: Tonkin 8,970, Annam 8,302, Cochinchina 1,305, total 18,577.
**Checked against the Annuaire itself (2026-11-10).** Vol. 11 (1943–46), PDF p. 42–43, printed p. 27–28,
"Tableau I. Superficie, population et densité de la population par province en 1946" (footnote 1: for
Annam and Tonkin the data are for 1943). I read the page image myself. Printed totals: Tonkin 10,687
villages (1943; the agent summed 27 provincial rows to the same figure); Annam 11,005 (1943); Cochinchina
223 cantons and 1,339 communes (1946, an estimate from the local government). 10,687 + 11,005 + 1,339 =
**23,031**, so the 1943 baseline is now a primary figure, with one caveat: the Cochinchina count is 1946,
and its 20 provincial rows sum to 1,356, not the printed 1,339 (a 17-unit gap in the table, unexplained).
The secondary study had three Annam figures wrong: Hà Tĩnh is 548 (not 513), Quảng Trị 484 (not 184),
Thừa Thiên 473 (not 173). The other coastal provinces match: Thanh Hóa 2,000, Nghệ An 927, Quảng Bình
383, Quảng Nam 1,065. Its "coastal 7,601" is not printed; the thirteen coastal provinces sum to 8,246
and the four highland provinces add 2,759. Same table, 1946: Cambodia 75 cantons, 996 communes; Laos 67
cantons, 8,910 communes. Vol. 11 has no village count for 1944, 1945 or 1946 in Tonkin or Annam.
Images: scratchpad `ocr-asi11/img/p-042.png`, `p-043.png` (not in the repo).

**Like-for-like, net, 1943 → 1957.**
- North: Tonkin plus Thanh Hóa to Quảng Bình is about 14,510 làng (12,800 on the xã basis).
  1957: 5,406. Net −63% (−58% on the xã basis).
- Whole country: 1943 is 23,031 (18,577); 1957 is the North's 5,406 plus the South's about 2,973,
  about 8,400. Net −64% (−55%).

Every pairing falls short of 2025's −67%. But this is net over fourteen years, with a partial reversal
in between (vol. 10: large communes split back), so the trough may be deeper than either endpoint,
and the only trough figure, 5,362 in 1949, does not cover the whole country. What 2025 has that 1946–50
does not: one nationwide legal act, one effective date (1 July 2025), no reversal.

**Claim shapes that survive.**
- Safe now: "the largest reduction in the number of commune-level units in a single nationwide
  reform", provided the 1946–50 mergers are described as piecemeal, province by province.
- Not safe: "the largest ever" without qualification. 1946–50 may match it.

**1976–79 (ruled out at commune level, with a caveat).** North 5,523 xã (end 1959). South: Ân vol. I
(RVN list, ~p. 756+) totals 47 provinces, 246 quận, 2,973 xã; the per-province lists date from
1957–64, so this is an approximation. That gives roughly 8,500 nationally around 1975, against 10,331
in the 1997 yearbook: a rise, not a fall. The North 1975 count is not in hand.

## The 1950 northern plates (Trung Châu Bắc Việt, 1:100,000)

VMA holds 15 rows (11 province plates, georeferenced; cover, legend, sheet index, composite) from
*Bản đồ hành chính Trung Châu Bắc Việt Nam*, Sở Địa-chính Bắc-Việt, 1950 (Rumsey 14320). The legend
draws province, district (quận), canton (tổng) and village (làng) limits and names each class.
Read on the Hưng Yên plate (2026-11-10, by eye, one reader): district names in spaced red capitals,
canton names underlined, village names in italics with dotted village limits. So in the zone this
publisher mapped, cantons and villages were still in use in 1950. That bears on the 1946 question:
the Democratic Republic's mergers do not show here. No OCR labels exist for these plates yet.

Pilot count, Phù Cừ district (Hưng Yên plate; Ân's end-of-French-period list: 6 tổng, 53 xã): about
45–55 village names fall in the area I read as Phù Cừ. This is an eyeball estimate, not a count: the
district limit is a dash-dot line I could follow only in part. It is consistent with no merger. It
would not catch a difference under about ten percent. A real count needs the district limit traced on
the georeferenced plate and the village labels read by machine, then checked by a second reader.

OCR pilot (same day, current pipeline, local only, nothing written to the database): `ocr.py batch`
on the Phù Cừ window (crop 5400,4100,3700,2300; 8 tiles of 1300 px, render 2048; run
`hungyen-phucu-pilot-261110`, output under `work/ocr/outputs/3e37c90d-…/runs/`). 220 unique labels:
188 village-type (mixed case), 22 canton or district names (capitals), 10 other. They cover the whole
window, which includes villages of neighbouring districts and of Hải Dương, so 188 is not Phù Cừ's
count. Recall check: of 45 village names I read off the image by eye, all 45 were in the OCR output
(accent-blind match at 0.85). That tests only names legible to me; it cannot show a name neither of us
saw. The district polygon is still the missing piece: nothing yet says which of the 188 are inside Phù Cừ.

Machine-drafted district limit (same day): the plate's 9 graticule points fit an affine to about 12 px
(about 75 m at 6.2 m/px) and the Luộc river lines up. The modern Phù Cừ outline (OSM, 1 January 2025
extract, admin level 6) was projected onto the plate and the three OCR runs (window plus top and bottom
strips, 244 village-type labels after dedup) counted against it: 57 inside, 44–69 if the edge is
trusted only to 500 m. **That 57 is not a result.** Drawn on the plate, the modern outline reaches the
Hải Dương border and the Luộc river, areas that look like Tiên Lữ in 1950, and leaves out the western
villages under the printed "PHÙ-CỪ" label. The district was redrawn after 1950. What it does show: the
count is nowhere near half of Ân's 53, which a doubling of commune size (vol. 10) would give.
Still needed: the 1950 limit itself, read off the dash-dot line on the plate.

Source families: Ân's Bắc Kỳ table (Hưng Yên: 8 units, 63 tổng, 510 xã) is probably the origin of the
National Archives' 8,970 xã for Bắc Kỳ, so these are one source, not two. The Annuaire's 10,687 counts
làng, which is more than xã.

## Annuaire statistique de l'Indochine, vol. 12 (1947–48)

A Sonnet agent OCRed about 69 pages (cap was 60) of the scan (317 pages, no text layer; PDF page =
printed page + 11). It found **no commune, village or canton count**: not in the contents, not in the
alphabetical index (PDF 304–317), not in Chapter II (PDF 26–37), not in spot checks of chapters X, XII
and XIII. Chapters I and III–XI were not read table by table. I checked one page image myself (PDF 32,
printed p. 21): Table 7, "Estimation de la population contrôlée dans le Nord Vietnam en 1948", lists 17
provinces, total 4,688,852; Hưng Yên 298,465; the Hà Nội and Hải Phòng rows include their suburbs
(footnote 3). That is the population the French side controlled in Tonkin in 1948, against 8.45
million Vietnamese in Tonkin in 1943 (a different measure, so only a rough ratio, about 55%). It shows
that a 1948 nationwide commune stock has to be built zone by zone: the Democratic Republic's 5,362
(1949) cannot be the whole country. Table 5 (Cochinchina, 23 rows) and Table 3 (country totals 1943
and 1948) are on PDF 30–31. Page images are under the scratchpad `ocr-asi12/img/` (not in the repo).
Annam has no table: the volume says there is no satisfactory estimate since 1943.

## So what

- Province tier: 1976 (−42%) is close to 2025 (−46%). The claim depends on the counting rule.
- Commune tier: the decisive window is 1946–50, and one number would settle it: the commune
  count in a year between 1947 and 1950 for every region, not only where the Democratic Republic
  governed. Sources: the Annuaire's 1943–46 volume (ANOM / SEADELT) and Ân vol. I's northern
  provincial lists after 1946. Still unread: 1980–89.
- Page images are owed for 23,031 (Annuaire, pp. 27–), 5,362 and 5,523 before any use.
- Sources: Ân and Tư are one family, so each Ân number needs a second source.
- Not found: NSO long series before 1997 (the 1980 yearbook is on seadelt.net, but the download
  link returned an error page). Wikipedia's commune page is a decree chronology with no stocks.
- Checks owed: the 5,362 and 5,523 are OCR text; confirm on the page images.
- Two versions of the 2025 composition exist: 2,621 xã + 687 phường + 13 đặc khu (Standing
  Committee) against 2,636 + 672 + 13 (Government plan). Both total 3,321.
