### Summary Assessment
From outside cartography and geodesy, the paper's central mechanism is immediately recognizable as an instance of a well-studied general problem in software verification: a test oracle that shares an assumption with the system under test cannot detect an error in that shared assumption, a phenomenon that motivates metamorphic testing and related oracle-design literature. The paper develops this insight rigorously and specifically for georeferenced map series without ever naming the general pattern, which is a missed opportunity — not a defect, since the paper's contribution stands on its own domain-specific evidence, but a strengthening opportunity that would broaden the paper's audience and let GIScience readers borrow directly from decades of software-testing practice on oracle design (e.g., differential testing, metamorphic relations, N-version programming) rather than reinventing analogous instruments from scratch. Practically, the paper's own incident is a useful stress test of its §9 recommendations: the archive that now recommends "begin with low-cost relations the series itself supplies" is the same archive whose original pipeline did not do that, and shipped a check with exactly the shared-input structure the paper now identifies as unsafe. The paper is candid about the algorithmic mechanism of that failure but silent on the process question — what allowed a shared-input design to ship unquestioned — which is the single most actionable lesson a practitioner-reader building a similar system would want. I would not withhold acceptance for this, but a paragraph in §9 or the discussion acknowledging that the taxonomy is itself a retrospective tool born from a failure to apply it prospectively would materially strengthen the paper's practical value and honesty.

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit
- **Explicit assumptions**: The paper is unusually explicit about its own assumptions throughout (e.g., "the CRS-selection check and comparison lattice share the adopted Helmert parameters," abstract) — a genuine strength that partly offsets the process-reflection gap noted above.
- **Implicit assumptions**: One implicit assumption worth surfacing: the paper assumes a reader has the engineering resources to build a seam census, lattice-collision detector, and independent-reference pipeline as described; this is not stated as a precondition for the §9 advice being actionable.
- **Paradigmatic assumptions**: None of concern; the paper does not over-claim a positivist "true position exists and is knowable" stance — it is careful to scope every claim to what the described checks can and cannot establish.

#### Cross-Disciplinary Connections
- **Parallel research**: Software-testing oracle-problem literature (differential testing, metamorphic testing, N-version programming) studies exactly this shared-assumption-blindness phenomenon in a different domain.
- **Borrowing opportunities**: The paper's Table 1 (checks × error classes) is structurally similar to a test-oracle capability matrix; framing it that way could make the taxonomy portable to readers building verification for other kinds of self-referential archival/geospatial pipelines (e.g., point-cloud registration, remote-sensing mosaic QA).
- **Methodological borrowing**: Metamorphic testing's practice of deriving *independent* metamorphic relations (transformations that should NOT preserve the property under test) parallels the paper's own distinction between a diagnostic left "unspent" (the seam census) versus one "spent as a constraint" (Janata & Cajthaml's adjustment) — the paper has independently rediscovered a version of this design principle.

#### Practical Impact
- **Real-world application**: High — any team maintaining a georeferenced series archive, digital-humanities map collection, or similar self-referential geospatial verification system will recognize the failure mode immediately.
- **Implementation feasibility**: The individual checks (seam census, lattice residual/collision, CRS-selection scoring) are each modestly resourced (the paper reports ~15 s/sheet for a comparable per-sheet check elsewhere in the literature), but a small project would need to build several of them; the paper does not estimate total engineering cost for adopting its §9 ordering.
- **Stakeholders**: Downstream *users* of the archive (researchers, historians, GIS practitioners relying on `maparchive.vn`) who consumed the displaced data before the fault was caught are not discussed — see Missing Stakeholder Perspectives below; the Devil's Advocate seat may also address this from the argument-integrity angle.

#### Broader Implications
- **Ethical dimensions**: Publishing a documented account of one's own system's fault, including the current unmeasurable production state (404 manifest), is a genuinely valuable act of transparency uncommon in software/data engineering; this should be acknowledged as a strength, not merely tolerated as a limitation.
- **Social impact**: Downstream researchers who used the archive's displaced coordinates during the affected window may have propagated the 455 m error into their own work; the paper does not address whether or how affected users were or will be notified.
- **Future directions**: A natural follow-up is a general-purpose "verification scope" checklist or library, applicable beyond map series (any self-referential geospatial or archival pipeline), which the paper's Table 1 already gestures toward but does not generalize into a reusable artifact.
